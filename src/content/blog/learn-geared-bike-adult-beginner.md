If you want to learn a bike as an adult, the first five classes decide a lot. That's where most grown-up beginners either relax into it or quietly decide motorcycles "aren't for them". This post walks through what bike training for beginners in Kolkata looks like in those early classes, so nothing surprises you.

Maybe you're tired of waiting for an auto at the stand, or you've ridden a scooty for years and want a proper bike. You already know the idea: clutch, gears, throttle. What worries you is doing them all at once with a 150 kg machine under you and a bus behind you. Good news: you won't be doing that in the first five classes.

For the full 15-class course outline, see our [bike training in Kolkata](/blog/bike-training-in-kolkata) post. This one zooms in on the start.

## Why adults find it harder (and why that's fine)

An adult thinks about everything: what if I stall, what if I drop it, what if someone's watching.

That caution isn't a weakness. It means you'll take corrections seriously. You just need classes that move in the right order, so your brain gets one new thing at a time.

## What to wear to your first class

Get this right before day one:

- **Closed shoes that cover the ankle.** Your left foot changes gears by pushing the lever up and pressing it down. In sandals or chappals that hurts, and the lever can catch your toes. Sports shoes work; ankle-covering boots are better.
- **Full-length trousers.** Jeans are fine. Shorts are not; the engine and silencer get hot.
- **A proper helmet.** An ISI-marked helmet that fits snugly. If you don't own one yet, ask us before your first class.
- **Gloves if you have them.** They help your grip.
- **Nothing loose.** Tuck in a dupatta or a long scarf. Anything that can reach the chain or wheel shouldn't be there.

## Bike training for beginners in Kolkata: your first 5 classes

Our [Bike Training](/courses/bike-training) runs 15 classes, one-to-one, on our practice bike. Classes 1 to 3 are about getting to know the bike, and classes 4 to 6 are about clutch and gears. Here's roughly how the first five go. Your trainer adjusts this to you, so treat it as a guide, not a timetable.

**Class 1: the bike stops being a stranger.**
You learn where everything is: clutch on the left hand, front brake on the right hand, gear lever under the left foot, rear brake under the right foot. You get on and off, take the bike off the side stand and put it back, and push it a few metres while walking beside it. That teaches you how the bike's weight moves, which is half the fear gone.

**Class 2: engine on, nothing moving.**
Starting the engine, finding neutral, watching the neutral light. Pulling the clutch in and letting it out slowly while the bike stays in neutral, just to feel the lever.

**Class 3: brakes and stopping.**
Both brakes, and how they work together. Your trainer may have you move very slowly and stop, again and again, so stopping becomes automatic before going ever does. That order is on purpose.

**Class 4: first gear, first metres.**
This is the one people remember. Clutch in, first gear, and then letting the clutch out slowly until the bike starts to creep forward. Then clutch in, brake, stop. You'll stall a few times. Everybody does.

**Class 5: moving off smoothly.**
Moving off and stopping until it stops feeling like an event. Short straight runs in first gear. Some learners touch second gear here; some don't. Either is normal.

## The clutch fear, honestly

Almost every adult beginner says the same thing: "I'm scared of the clutch." Here's what's actually going on.

The clutch lever connects and disconnects the engine from the rear wheel. Pull it in fully and the engine can't push the bike at all. That makes the clutch your **safety lever**, not your enemy. Anything feels wrong? Pull the clutch in and brake. The bike stops being pushed forward.

Stalling feels dramatic but is harmless. The engine stops, the bike jerks a little and halts. You pull the clutch in, find neutral, start again. No damage, no shame.

What takes practice is letting the clutch out slowly at the right spot. That's a skill your hand learns through repetition, not something you can learn from reading. Your trainer will spend real time on it. If stalling is already your problem, our post on the [clutch biting point](/blog/bike-clutch-biting-point-stalling) has a simple drill.

## Coming from a scooty?

You have a head start. Your balance, throttle hand and road sense already work. What's new is your left hand and left foot. On our homepage there's a learner who started on a scooty on Day 4 and was riding a geared bike by Day 15. It's a common path.

One thing to unlearn: on a scooty, the left lever is the rear brake. On a bike, it's the clutch. In the first classes, your hand will try to brake with it. Your trainer will catch it.

## Questions people ask

**Am I too old to learn a geared bike?**
We teach adults of all ages. What matters more is that you're reasonably fit, can handle the bike's weight at a standstill, and come to classes regularly. Read our post on [learning at any age](/blog/age-to-learn-scooty-licence-rules).

**Do I need to know a cycle first?**
For a geared bike, basic cycle balance helps a lot. If you can't ride a cycle, starting with our [Basic Scooty Training](/courses/basic-scooty-training) first is often the smoother route.

**Will I ride on the road in the first five classes?**
No. The first classes happen on quiet ground. Road riding comes once clutch, gear and throttle work together.
