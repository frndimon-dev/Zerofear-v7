Looking for scooty training in Behala? Then you already know the problem: there is nowhere quiet to start. Ask anyone in Sakherbazar or Thakurpukur and you hear the same thing: "Our para lanes are full of parked cars, and the main road is Diamond Harbour Road."

This page is for you. It covers where Behala learners practise with us, how long the trip takes, and when it makes more sense for the trainer to come to your door.

## Scooty training in Behala: why the first ride is hard

Diamond Harbour Road runs the whole length of Behala, from Taratala down past Behala Chowrasta, Sakherbazar and Thakurpukur towards Joka. It is the spine of the area, and it is busy almost all day. Buses pull in and out of stops without warning, autos stop wherever a passenger waves, and the Purple Line metro pillars along the road mean lanes narrow and shift in places.

James Long Sarani on the western side is a little calmer, but it still has its own bus traffic, school rush in the morning and a crowded market stretch in the evening.

The inside roads are where most Behala learners first try on a relative's scooty. Parnasree, the lanes behind Sakherbazar, the paras off Thakurpukur. They look quiet until a car is parked on one side, a rickshaw is coming down the middle, and a cycle shoots out of a gali. For someone on day one, that is three things to watch at once. Most people put a foot down, panic, and park the scooty for months.

That's why we don't start anyone on their own street. We start on a quiet practice stretch, and we bring you back to roads like yours later, with the trainer beside you.

## Getting to us from Behala

Our centre is at 12, Samargarh Colony, near Paschim Putiary, a short walk from **Netaji Metro (Kudghat)** on the Blue Line. From most of Behala it is roughly 3.5 to 4.5 km, depending on which side you live on.

| Starting from | Simple way to reach us |
|---|---|
| Behala Chowrasta, Parnasree | Auto or cab east towards Tollygunge, then a short ride or walk to Kudghat |
| Sakherbazar, Thakurpukur | Cab or bike taxi via Haridevpur towards Kudghat, usually the quickest |
| Taratala side | Reach Tollygunge (Mahanayak Uttam Kumar) and take the Blue Line one stop south to Netaji |

A few practical points from Behala learners:

- **Avoid the 9 to 10:30 am and 6 to 8 pm rush on DH Road** if you're coming by bus. A 20-minute trip can turn into 45.
- **Evening classes after office** are possible. Leave a bit earlier than you think during puja season, when Behala's pandal crowds spill onto the main road.
- **In the monsoon**, the low stretches around Behala and Thakurpukur waterlog fast. Call us before you leave on a heavy-rain day and we'll tell you if the practice lane is fit to ride.

## Doorstep training in Behala

If travelling to Kudghat isn't practical, your trainer can come to you. Behala is one of the areas we often cover for [doorstep training](/doorstep-training), along with Thakurpukur and Haridevpur.

Doorstep fees start at ₹4,500 and depend on your distance from Netaji Metro. Most of Behala is beyond 3 km, so the fee rises with distance, up to a maximum of ₹8,000. The calculator on the doorstep page shows your exact amount, or you can send us your area on WhatsApp.

Doorstep makes sense if you look after children or elderly parents, or if you simply want to learn on the roads you'll actually use. Your trainer will still look for the quietest stretch near you for the first few classes. The busy roads come later.

## What you can learn

- **[Basic Scooty Training](/courses/basic-scooty-training):** 15 classes for complete beginners, including people who never rode a cycle.
- **[Advanced Scooty Training](/courses/advanced-scooty-training):** 10 classes for people who can ride in an empty lane but freeze at a junction or behind a bus. Good preparation for DH Road.
- **[Bike Training](/courses/bike-training):** 15 classes on a geared bike, from clutch and gears to road riding.

Every class is one-to-one. Practice vehicles are provided, or you can bring your own scooty. Many of our women learners ask about trainers and timings first; the [women riders page](/women-riders) answers that.

## The Behala road you'll ride at the end

Here is what we aim for. By the end of the basic course, you should be able to pull out of your lane, wait for a gap, join the left side of a road like James Long Sarani, ride past a stopped bus without swerving, and turn back into your para. Your first real DH Road ride will be with a trainer behind you, at a quiet hour, on a short stretch. Then a longer one.
