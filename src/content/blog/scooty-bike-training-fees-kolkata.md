## What the fee covers

- **One-to-one classes.** One trainer, one learner. Nobody waits in a queue while you practise.
- **Our practice vehicle.** Scooty or bike, kept in good condition. If you'd rather learn on your own scooty, that's fine too.
- **Ground first, then the road.** Balance and control on a quiet stretch near Netaji Metro, then real lanes, then normal traffic once you're ready.
- **No rush.** If you need an extra class or two for one skill, your trainer slows down. We'd rather you leave confident than leave on schedule.

What it doesn't cover: your learner's licence or driving licence fees. Those are paid to the transport department, not to us. Our [RTO test tips](/blog/rto-two-wheeler-test-tips) explain what the test asks.

## Why one cheap class can cost more

You'll find someone who will "teach" for less: a cousin, a neighbour, a friend with a free Sunday. Many of our learners tried that first. The usual story goes like this: a few rushed sessions, one wobble, a scare near a parked car, and then six months of not touching the scooty again.

A course is paid practice with someone whose only job is to watch what your hands and feet are doing and fix it early. Bad habits are easy to learn and hard to undo, like grabbing the front brake in a turn or putting a foot down every time you slow. Unlearning them later costs more classes than learning right the first time.
