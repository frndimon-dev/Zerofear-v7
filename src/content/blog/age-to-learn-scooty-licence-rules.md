Two calls we get often. One is a parent: "My son is 16, can he start scooty classes?" The other is someone in their fifties, a little embarrassed: "Am I too old for this?" Both are really asking about the right age to learn scooty, and the answers come from two different places.

The lower limit is set by law, through the licence. The upper limit isn't set by anyone. It depends on you, and it's much higher than most people think.

## Age for two wheeler licence: what the law says

The Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 sets the minimum age for driving on a public road. In simple terms:

| Age | What the law allows |
|---|---|
| Under 16 | No licence. Can't ride on a public road. |
| 16 and 17 | Only a gearless two-wheeler with an engine of 50 cc or less, and the learner licence needs a parent's or guardian's written consent |
| 18 and above | Learner and driving licence for scooties (MCWOG) and geared bikes (MCWG) |

There's a catch in the middle row. Almost every scooty sold today has an engine well above 50 cc. So in practice, for an ordinary scooty, **you have to be 18** before you can get a learner licence and ride it on the road.

There's no upper age limit for a two-wheeler licence. If you're 40 or older, you'll usually need a medical certificate (Form 1A) signed by a registered doctor when you apply. The Sarathi portal tells you what applies to you.

Rules change, so check the latest on the official Sarathi portal, **sarathi.parivahan.gov.in**, before you apply. For the full process from learner licence to driving test, read our [two-wheeler licence steps](/blog/two-wheeler-licence-kolkata-steps).

## If you're 16 or 17

We understand. Friends are riding and the scooty is right there in the garage. But the law has become much stricter on underage riding since the 2019 amendment to the Motor Vehicles Act.

If someone under 18 is caught breaking the Act, the parent, guardian or owner of the vehicle can be held guilty. The penalty can include a fine of ₹25,000 and up to three years in jail, the vehicle's registration can be cancelled for a year, and the young rider can be barred from getting a licence until the age of 25.

Our honest advice: if you're 17, start learning the road from the pillion seat. Watch how the rider slows before a turn. Learn the road signs; you'll need them for the learner test anyway. Then, when you turn 18, apply for the learner licence and take classes.

## In your 20s and 30s

Here life usually forces the decision: a new job in Sector V, a college across town, a child's school run. The problem at this age isn't learning. It's time.

Classes are about 30 minutes, and evening classes after office hours are possible. What matters most is regularity. Three classes in a week stick far better than three classes spread over a month. ## At 40, 50, 60: real advice for older learners

Nothing in the rules stops you. A few things do change, and it helps to plan for them instead of worrying about them.

**Get your eyes checked first.** Riding needs good distance vision, and your licence application will ask about it anyway. If you need glasses, ride with them.

**Pick a light scooty with an easy step-through.** A scooty is far kinder to stiff knees and a sore back than a geared bike. You don't have to swing a leg over a fuel tank, and you don't have to hold a clutch. Better still, learn on our practice scooty first and buy later.

**Expect the first classes to feel slow. That's normal.** You'll think before every move, and then one day it clicks. Fear plays a bigger part than age. People who had a fall years ago take longer, and we go slowly with them on purpose.

**Ride with more room.** Reaction time slows a little as we get older. The fix is simple: ride a bit slower, keep more distance from the vehicle in front, and avoid N.S.C. Bose Road at 6 pm until you're truly comfortable.

**Ask your doctor about your medicines.** Some medicines cause drowsiness or affect balance.

**Avoid the hottest part of the day.** A Kolkata afternoon in May drains anyone. Morning or evening classes are easier on the body and the mind.

Your licence also renews more often as you get older. Under the 2019 amendment, a licence issued before 30 is valid until 40; between 30 and 50 it's valid for 10 years; between 50 and 55, until you turn 60; and after 55, for five years at a time. Again, Sarathi has the current rules.

## Questions people ask

**What's the minimum age to learn a scooty in India?**
For an ordinary scooty, 18, because you need a learner licence to ride on the road and most scooties are over 50 cc. At 16, the law allows only a gearless two-wheeler of 50 cc or less, with a guardian's written consent.

**Is 55 too old to learn?**
No. There's no upper age limit for a licence. Plan for a medical certificate, take your classes regularly, and give yourself a little extra time.

**Can I learn without knowing how to ride a cycle, at my age?**
Yes. A scooty doesn't need cycling skills. Read [how we teach people who never rode a cycle](/blog/learn-scooty-without-cycle).
