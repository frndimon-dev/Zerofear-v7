Most people who can't ride a cycle ride a scooty on their own in about **15 classes**. Some get there in 10. A few need 18 or 20. It's counted in weeks, not months.

That's the honest answer. The rest of this page explains what actually happens in those classes, so you know what to expect before you start.

## Why "without a cycle" isn't the problem you think

A cycle asks you to balance and pedal at the same time, at a speed where falling feels likely. A scooty has no pedals. Your feet stay free, close to the ground, and the scooty moves only when your right hand asks it to.

So the real job isn't "learn balance from zero". It's three smaller skills:

1. a gentle throttle hand,
2. steady eyes, looking where you want to go, not at the front wheel,
3. feet that come up once you're moving and go down only when you stop.

## The timeline, class by class

This is how our [Basic Scooty Training](/courses/basic-scooty-training) runs. Each class is about 30 minutes, one-to-one.

**Classes 1 to 3: the scooty stops being scary.**
You sit, hold the handle, find both brakes, and feel what a tiny twist of the throttle does. Your trainer walks beside you and holds the scooty. Most people go from gripping the handle hard to sitting relaxed by the third class.

**Classes 4 to 6: balance.**
Straight lines at walking pace, feet close to the ground. Then the moment everyone remembers: the first stretch with both feet up. One of our learners said it on camera on her ninth day: *"Aaj paa tulte perechi."* Today I lifted my feet. Some people get it on day 4, some on day 9, and both are normal.

**Classes 7 to 9: turns.**
Left and right turns, slowing down before the turn instead of braking in it, and using the indicator. This is where people who learned from a friend usually get stuck, so we take our time here.

**Classes 10 to 12: quiet roads.**
Real lanes around Putiary and Kudghat with parked cars, a rickshaw coming the other way, and a dog that won't move. Your trainer stays with you.

**Classes 13 to 15: on your own.**
Your trainer gives less help and you do more. By the end, you start, ride, turn and stop on a normal road without anyone telling you what to do.

## What makes it faster or slower

- **Practising on consecutive days.** Three classes in one week stick better than three classes spread across three weeks.
- **Fear, not age.** We've taught college students and grandmothers. The ones who take longer are the ones who had a bad fall before, and that's exactly why we go slowly with them.
- **Height.** If your feet don't reach the ground flat, we teach you to balance on your toes and pick a lower seat. It adds a class or two, not a month.
- **Skipping the basics.** People who want to "go on the road on day 2" usually lose more time than they save.

## "My friend learned in 3 days"

Maybe they did. Some people already know a cycle, or have sat on a scooty for years as a pillion. A learner in one of our feedback videos said she was riding on her own after two or three classes. But "riding" in an empty lane and riding through Tollygunge at 6 pm are different things. We count you as done when you can do the second one.

## So how long, really?

Plan on **15 classes of about 30 minutes**. If you can come regularly, that's roughly three to five weeks. If you need more, we add classes; we don't push you out half-ready.
