Getting a two-wheeler driving licence in Kolkata happens in two stages: first a learner licence, then the permanent driving licence after a test on the road. People lose weeks by picking the wrong vehicle class or letting the learner licence run out.

Here is the whole process in order. Rules and fees change from time to time, so check the latest on the official Sarathi portal (sarathi.parivahan.gov.in) before you apply.

## Step 0: Choose the right class

Your licence lists the kinds of vehicle you may drive. For two-wheelers there are two you'll see on Sarathi:

| Class | What it covers |
|---|---|
| MCWOG | Motorcycle without gear: a scooty |
| MCWG | Motorcycle with gear: a geared bike |

If you will only ever ride a scooty, MCWOG is the class you need. If you want to ride a geared bike, apply for MCWG. If you might ride both, you can select both classes in the same application, and you'll be tested on each. It's much easier to add a class now than to come back for it later.

## Step 1: Check your age

- **18 years and above:** you can apply for MCWOG and MCWG.
- **16 to 18 years:** the Motor Vehicles Act allows a licence only for a gearless two-wheeler with an engine of **50 cc or less**, and the learner licence needs written consent from a parent or guardian. Most scooties sold today have bigger engines than that, so for a normal scooty you have to be 18.

## Step 2: Apply for the learner licence on Sarathi

Go to sarathi.parivahan.gov.in, choose **West Bengal**, and open the learner licence application. You'll fill in your details, choose the vehicle class, upload documents, pay the fee online and book a slot for the learner licence test.

Documents you'll typically need:

- **Proof of age** and **proof of address.** Aadhaar is the one most people use, and linking it makes the online process smoother.
- **A recent photo and your signature**, uploaded in the size the portal asks for.
- **A declaration of physical fitness (Form 1).** Some applicants, for example older applicants, also need a **medical certificate in Form 1A** signed by a registered doctor. Sarathi tells you if it applies to you.

Fees differ by service and number of classes. Check the current fee on Sarathi when you apply rather than trusting a figure from a blog, including this one.

## Step 3: Pass the learner licence test

The learner test is a short computer-based test of multiple-choice questions on traffic rules, road signs and safe driving. Depending on what Sarathi offers for West Bengal when you apply, you'll take it at the RTO slot you booked or online.

Practise with the mock tests on Sarathi. Road signs are where most people slip, so spend an evening on them.

Once you pass, you can download your learner licence.

## Step 4: Ride on your learner licence, properly

Your learner licence is **valid for six months** from the date it's issued. During that time you can practise on the road, under the conditions printed on it. Put an **"L" sign** on the front and back of the vehicle, wear a helmet, and don't treat it as a licence to carry friends around.

## Step 5: Wait at least 30 days, then book the driving test

Under the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, you can appear for the driving test only after you've held your learner licence for **at least 30 days**. You must also apply before the learner licence expires at six months.

So your window is roughly **day 31 to month 6**. Don't leave it to the last week. Test slots can be booked up, and if your learner licence lapses you generally have to apply for a new one and take the learner test again.

Apply for the driving licence on Sarathi, pay the fee, and book a test slot at the RTO for your address.

## Step 6: The driving test

Arrive early with your learner licence, application papers and ID. You'll usually need to bring a vehicle of the class you're testing for, with its registration, insurance and pollution papers in order, and a helmet. Check what your RTO asks for when you book.

The two-wheeler test checks control at low speed. The best-known part is the **figure 8**, ridden slowly without putting a foot down, along with smooth starts and stops, using indicators and looking around before you turn. If you don't pass, you can apply to take the test again after a waiting period, as long as your learner licence is still valid. Sarathi shows the retest rules and fee.

## Step 7: Collect your two-wheeler driving licence

Once you pass, the licence is processed and the card is sent to your address. You can also keep a digital copy through DigiLocker or mParivahan. Track the status on Sarathi using your application number.

## Questions people ask

**Can I ride a scooty with an MCWG licence?**
Classes are separate on paper. If you'll ride both a scooty and a geared bike, the simplest way to stay on the right side of the rules is to have both MCWOG and MCWG on your licence.

**How long does the whole thing take?**
At least a month, because of the 30-day rule, and usually a bit more depending on test slots.

**I'm 17. Can I get a scooty licence?**
Only for a gearless two-wheeler up to 50 cc, with a guardian's written consent. For a regular scooty, wait until you turn 18.
