People searching for **bike training in Gariahat** usually tell us one of two things. Either they want to stop depending on autos, or they already ride a scooty and want to move to a geared bike. Both are doable from this part of the city, but the roads here ask for skills a quiet lane never teaches.

We've written before about [learning a scooty near Rabindra Sarobar](/blog/scooty-training-near-rabindra-sarobar) and the calm lanes around the lake. This post is about everything around it: the crossings, the bridge and the stop-start traffic that make Gariahat and Ballygunge harder, and how to get ready for them.

## Why these roads are hard for a new rider

**Gariahat crossing.** Rash Behari Avenue and Gariahat Road meet in one of the busiest junctions in south Kolkata. Shoppers cross wherever they like, hawkers fill the pavements so people walk on the road, buses stop at the crossing, and during the Puja shopping weeks the whole stretch slows to a crawl. You spend most of your time between first and second gear, starting and stopping.

**Dhakuria bridge.** The bridge over the railway on Gariahat Road has a slope on both sides. Stopping on the way up in a jam and then starting again without rolling back is a real test, especially on a geared bike.

**Southern Avenue and Rash Behari Avenue.** Wide and fast when they're clear, then suddenly packed. Cars change lanes without warning, and buses pull out from stops.

**Prince Anwar Shah Road.** Heavy traffic for much of the day, with vehicles turning in and out of side roads and the big malls along it.

**Lake Gardens, Golf Green and Jodhpur Park.** Quieter residential lanes, but narrow, with cars parked on both sides and blind corners. Good for slow control practice, tricky for anyone who can't balance at walking pace.

## Bike training for Gariahat traffic: clutch control is everything

If you're learning a geared bike, Gariahat traffic is mostly a clutch test. Our [Bike Training](/courses/bike-training) course is 15 one-to-one classes, and it's built for exactly this:

- **Classes 1 to 3:** get to know the bike, its weight, the controls.
- **Classes 4 to 6:** clutch and gears, on their own.
- **Classes 7 to 9:** clutch, gear and throttle together, smoothly.
- **Then road riding**, step by step, with the trainer beside you.

The skill that matters most here is the friction zone: letting the clutch out just enough that the bike creeps forward at walking pace without stalling. Master that and the crossing stops being scary. A bike that stalls on the Dhakuria bridge slope, with buses behind, is what shakes most new riders. We practise the half-clutch start on a slope until it's boring.

One of our learners started on a scooty on Day 4 and was riding a geared bike by Day 15. Scooty first, then bike, is a route that works well.

## If you're learning a scooty

The [Basic Scooty Training](/courses/basic-scooty-training) course is 15 classes for complete beginners, even if you can't ride a cycle. For Gariahat, the habits that matter most are slow balance in a crawl, looking ahead instead of at the front wheel, and keeping your speed down near the bus stops.

If you can ride but Gariahat crossing makes you freeze, [Advanced Scooty Training](/courses/advanced-scooty-training) is 10 classes on busy roads, junctions, speed breakers and defensive riding.

## Questions people ask

**Can I learn a geared bike without ever riding a scooty?**
Yes. The Bike Training course starts from zero. Some people prefer scooty first, but it isn't required.

**Is the Dhakuria bridge part of the training?**
Slope starts are part of road practice once you're ready. We never take a beginner into heavy traffic early.

**Do I need a licence?**
To ride on public roads while learning, you need a learner's licence for the right vehicle class. Geared bikes and gearless scooties are separate classes. Check the latest rules and fees on Sarathi (sarathi.parivahan.gov.in).
